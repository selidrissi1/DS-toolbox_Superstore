Q1 · Which sub-categories sell well but earn little?

Strong sales = at or above the median sub-category. Weak profit = margin below the store's 12.5%.

5 of 17 sub-categories bring 46% of sales at a 2.8% margin

Flagged: Chairs, Storage, Tables, Machines, Bookcases.

They lose $91.0k on discounted lines that lose money

In the flagged groups, lines discounted up to 20% earn $108.1k; lines above 20% lose $78.1k. Binders loses the most ($38.5k) but is not flagged: its overall margin (14.9%) stays above the store's.

Q2 · Where does profit break, when to test, what to keep?

Profit turns negative above 20% discount; for Storage and Tables already at 20%

Whole store margin by band: 29.5% with no discount, 11.9% at 1-20%, -15.3% at 21-40%, -77.4% above 40%. Units per order line stay flat (3.8, 3.7, 3.8, 3.9): deeper discounts do not sell more units per line.

Sales peak in Sep, Nov, Dec (1.6-1.8x an average month), yet discounts stay at 14.6%-16.5% all year

Peak months are the time to test smaller discounts: demand is strongest, and the extra volume gives a faster answer. Feb is the quietest month (0.3x).

Few loss-making discounts are rescued by the basket: 5% (Machines) to 24% (Chairs)

Basket rescue rate: of the orders holding a discounted line that loses money, the share still profitable overall. 51% of orders have a single line, so most discounts cannot pull in other products.

Recommendation

Where: stop discounts above 20% in the flagged sub-categories, and test 10% where 20% already loses money or leaves a thin margin. When: run the tests in Sep, Nov, Dec. Keep: discounts up to 20% where the margin stays healthy; basket rescue is too low to justify loss-making discounts.
